# CEFR Assessment

Evaluate the English fluency of the user's text and assign one CEFR level.

## Step 1: Analyze fluency indicators
- Flow: does the text read smoothly, or feel choppy and disjointed?
- Sentence variety: is there a mix of simple and complex structures, or are patterns repetitive?
- Word choice: are vocabulary and expressions natural, or translation-like and stilted?
- Cohesion: do ideas connect logically with appropriate transitions?

## Step 2: Assign ONE level
- A1 (Beginner): very basic, fragmented sentences; high-frequency vocabulary only; frequent errors impede understanding.
- A2 (Elementary): simple sentences with some coordination; basic vocabulary; meaning is clear despite errors.
- B1 (Intermediate): connected text with some variety; can express familiar topics; errors are noticeable but don't obscure meaning.
- B2 (Upper Intermediate): clear, detailed text with good control; variety of structures; occasional unnatural phrasing.
- C1 (Advanced): well-structured, fluent text; natural expression; sophisticated vocabulary; minimal unnatural elements.
- C2 (Proficient): effortless, precise expression; nuanced and idiomatic; indistinguishable from a skilled native writer.

## Output format
CEFR Level: [Level]
Justification: [1–2 sentences on the key evidence for this level]
Key strengths: [1 short bullet]
Key improvement area: [1 short bullet]
